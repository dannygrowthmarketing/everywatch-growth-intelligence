The Calibre

Five complications, one calibre.

A calibre is the agreed-on mechanism — the architecture that the work runs on. Five complications cooperating under one plate: each performing one function, none redundant, all measurable. What follows is the case-back view.

Calibre EW.2425 — case-back view technical drawing · 1:1
i.
01 · Mainspring

Organic / SEO

The stored energy of the engagement. 41.3M lifetime organic impressions across category pages and editorial features. Long-tail watch references indexed and ranked; the engine that keeps running between paid pushes.

ii.
02 · Going train

Paid / SEM + social

Where stored energy is transmitted. Google Search + PMax + Meta. $23.2K paid spend producing $787.9K attributed revenue — the lifetime gear ratio is what made the rest viable.

iii.
03 · Escapement

Conversion / CRO

The regulator of release. Tick by tick: search-to-listing, listing-to-watchlist, watchlist-to-account. Subscription funnel friction mapped and removed at every escape; the watch keeps time because this part doesn't.

iv.
04 · Power reserve

Lifecycle / CRM

What the engagement holds in reserve. Saved-search alerts, auction notifications, watchlist nudges. The lifecycle engine compounds the cost-per-acquisition downward, month over month — the gauge that quietly fills.

v.
05 · Regulator

Analytics / Looker

The fine adjustment. GA4 + GTM + Looker Studio. A weekly observatory read showing rate variation across positions — what's running fast, what's losing time, where to fine-tune next.

The Lots

Three decisions, opened up.

case studies · auction-lot format

A catalogue lives or dies on its lot descriptions. Three from this engagement, each set out the way an auction house would: a description, a condition report, a provenance line, an estimate, and the figure realized when the hammer fell.

LOT · 11
Lot 11 · Acquisition · Lifetime

A full-funnel acquisition program, executed across paid and organic channels.

A $23.2K paid budget deployed across Google Search, PMax, and Meta — paired with a long-tail SEO programme producing 41.3M cumulative organic impressions. Lifetime ROAS of 34×. Peak month (May 2025) registered 105× — the year's "complete watch" reading.

ObservationPaid was set up but underperforming; SEO indexed but flat.
HypothesisQuality score & landing relevance, not bids, set the ceiling.
ValidationRebuilt SKAGs by reference family; rewrote landings; matched query.
DecisionDefunded broad terms; concentrated on intent-tail by brand & reference.
Result34× lifetime ROAS · 105× peak · 1.33M new users.
RecommendationHold ratio at >25× lifetime; treat 105× as ceiling, not floor.
Estimate · 10–15×

LOT · 27
Lot 27 · Organic · Long-tail

An editorial-search programme tuned to reference-level intent.

Every collectible has a reference number; every reference has a search history. We mapped 1,200+ long-tail reference queries onto landing pages with embedded transaction history, comparable sales, and current listings. The mainspring of the calibre — quiet, continuous, compound.

ObservationGeneric category pages ranked; reference-level intent leaked to forums.
HypothesisA reference page that answers the actual question wins both rank & conversion.
ValidationBuilt 280 reference pages; measured impression share & CTR by family.
DecisionProductionised the template; SEO becomes a publishing workflow, not a project.
Result41.3M lifetime impressions; long-tail CTR 3.1× category benchmark.
RecommendationExtend by complication (perpetual calendar, chronograph, etc.) before brand.

LOT · 43
Lot 43 · Lifecycle · Saved-search

A lifecycle automation built around saved-search and auction alerts.

Saved searches converted a one-time visitor into a returning watchlist owner. Email + on-site nudges fired only when a matching listing surfaced. Returning-user share rose from 18% to 47% across the engagement — measured carefully, given the October 2024 reading (see Folio V).

ObservationAuction-style buyers visit, search, and leave; few accounts created at first session.
HypothesisA saved-search alert is the only justifiable reason for a watch buyer to subscribe.
ValidationA/B'd the "save this search" CTA position; instrumented downstream return rate.
DecisionMade saved-search a first-class object; built the matching engine; emails on match.
ResultReturning-user share: 18% → 47%. CAC payback ~½ of pre-period.
RecommendationPromote auction-house-style "rare lot" notifications next; pair with seller fees.
Estimate · 30% return

Folio V
V.
The Caveat

Two readings still to be regulated.

data integrity notes

A catalogue raisonné that wouldn't tell you what's not yet settled isn't a catalogue, it's a brochure. Two open items — both surfaced, both work-in-progress, neither material to the headline but both worth resolving before this document leaves the editing room.

Caveat · 01

An anomalously low returning-user reading for October 2024.

The returning-user series shows a single-month dip in October 2024 that does not match the surrounding trajectory. Three candidates: a GA4 instrumentation gap during a tag-manager redeploy in early October; a one-off cohort effect (seasonal auction lull); or a genuine retention regression masked by subsequent recovery.

Resolution: cross-reference with subscription-product DB during next data review; do not cite October '24 as headline figure until reconciled.
Caveat · 02

"Attributed revenue" — definition still to be tightened.

The $787.9K figure used throughout is the attributed revenue per the Looker model — last-non-direct click within a 30-day window. Whether this reflects actual transacted GMV, qualified-lead value, or pipeline value depends on which EveryWatch product line is in scope (auction-house listing fees vs. dealer subscriptions vs. data API).

Resolution: split attribution by product line in next edition; show 7-, 30-, and 90-day windows in parallel.
